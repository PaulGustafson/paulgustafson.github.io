\section{Linear Algebra}

Linear algebra studies linear maps between vector spaces. A \textbf{vector space} $V$ over a field $k$ is a set equipped with vector addition and scalar multiplication operations satisfying straightforward compatibility conditions. A \textbf{linear map} $f\colon V \to U$ between $k$-vector spaces is a map of underlying sets such that $f(\alpha v + \beta w) = \alpha f(v) + \beta f(w)$ for all $\alpha, \beta \in k$ and $v, w \in V$. Examples include differential operators, group representations, coefficient matrices of linear systems of equations, and local approximations of globally nonlinear functions.

The \textbf{free $k$-vector space on a set $X$}, denoted $kX$, is the set of finitely supported set-theoretical functions $X \to k$ with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. The \textbf{direct sum} vector space $V \oplus W$ is the set $V \times W$ with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication defined by $\alpha (v \oplus w) = \alpha v \oplus \alpha w$. The \textbf{quotient} of $V$ by a subspace $W$, denoted $V / W$, is the set of equivalence classes of $V$ modulo the relation $x \sim y$ iff $x - y \in W$. The \textbf{tensor product} $V \otimes W$ is the vector space $k(V \times W) / N$ where $N$ is the subspace generated by the linearity relations on $V$ and $W$.

\begin{exercise} An \textbf{isomorphism} of vector spaces $V \cong W$ consists of a pair of linear maps $f\colon V \to W$ and $g\colon W \to V$ such that $f \circ g = 1_W$ and $g \circ f = 1_V$. Prove the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item $k(X \sqcup Y) \cong kX \oplus kY$.
\item $k(X \times Y) \cong kX \otimes kY$.
\item The first isomorphism theorem for vector spaces (and the second and third if desired).
\item Find universal properties determining up to canonical isomorphism the free $k$-vector space, direct sum, quotient, tensor product, and dual space (defined below), respectively.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

The \textbf{dual space} $V^*$ is the space of linear maps from a vector space $V$ to its ground field $k$. The evaluation map $\ev_V\colon V^* \otimes V \to k$ is the linear extension of $f \otimes x \mapsto f(x)$. A linear map $T\colon V\to V$ induces the dual map $T^*\colon V^*\to V^*$ defined by $T^*(\phi)=\phi\circ T$.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $T\colon V\to V$ describe the evolution of a linear system. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item A linear observable $\phi\in V^*$ is conserved iff $T^*\phi=\phi$.
\item The canonical map $V\to V^{**}$ sends a state to the rule assigning to each observable its value on that state.
\item A state is determined by the values of all linear observables when $V$ is finite dimensional.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

The tensor product introduced above is the state space of a composite linear system.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $V$ and $W$ be state spaces of two systems. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Bilinear observables on $V\times W$ correspond canonically to linear observables on $V\otimes W$.
\item Not every element of $V\otimes W$ is of the form $v\otimes w$ when $\dim(V),\dim(W)>1$.
\item For $\psi\in V\otimes W$, contraction defines a map $\widetilde{\psi}\colon V^*\to W$, and $\psi$ is decomposable iff $\widetilde{\psi}$ has rank at most one.
\end{itemize}
Non-decomposable tensors represent entangled states.
\end{exercise}

We say that $V$ is \textbf{finite dimensional} if there is a coevaluation morphism $\coev_V\colon k \to V \otimes V^*$ satisfying the snake equations
\[
(1_V \otimes \ev_V) \circ (\coev_V \otimes 1_V)=1_V,
\qquad
(\ev_V \otimes 1_{V^*}) \circ (1_{V^*} \otimes \coev_V)=1_{V^*}.
\]
For $f\in\End(V)$, define
\[
\trace(f)=(\ev_V \circ s_{V,V^*} \circ (f \otimes 1_{V^*}) \circ \coev_V)(1),
\]
where $s_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X$ is the symmetric braiding $x\otimes y\mapsto y\otimes x$. Define $\dim(V)=\trace(1_V)$.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $V$ and $W$ be finite dimensional. For $F\in\End(V\otimes W)$, use evaluation, coevaluation, and the symmetric braiding to define}
\[
\trace_W(F)=(1_V\otimes\ev_W)\circ(1_V\otimes s_{W,W^*})
\circ(F\otimes1_{W^*})\circ(1_V\otimes\coev_W).
\]
\textit{Prove that this gives a map $\trace_W\colon\End(V\otimes W)\to\End(V)$, that it is natural under isomorphisms of $V$ and $W$, and that}
\[
\trace_W(f\otimes g)=\trace(g)f.
\]
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise} \textit{Prove the following for finite-dimensional vector spaces $V,W$, endomorphisms $f,g\in\End(V)$, and $h\in\End(W)$:}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\trace(fg)=\trace(gf)$.
\item $\trace(f\oplus h)=\trace(f)+\trace(h)$.
\item $\trace(f\otimes h)=\trace(f)\trace(h)$.
\item $\dim(V)\in\mathbb{N}$.
\item $V\cong W$ iff $\dim(V)=\dim(W)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

Suppose $k=\mathbb{C}$. A Hermitian inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon V\times V\to\mathbb{C}$ is a positive-definite sesquilinear form, linear in the first variable, conjugate linear in the second variable, and conjugate symmetric.

\begin{exercise} \textit{For a nonzero vector $\psi$ in a finite-dimensional Hermitian vector space $V$, define}
\[
p_\psi(v)=\frac{\langle v,\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle}\psi.
\]
\textit{Prove that $p_\psi$ is a self-adjoint idempotent of rank one and depends only on the one-dimensional subspace spanned by $\psi$. Conclude that a pure state is naturally a ray rather than a distinguished vector.}
\end{exercise}

A linear map $f\colon V\to V$ is \textbf{self-adjoint} if $\langle fv,w\rangle=\langle v,fw\rangle$ for all $v,w\in V$.

\begin{exercise}
\textit{Prove that any self-adjoint linear map $f$ on a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$ is diagonalizable:}
\[
f=\sum_i\lambda_i p_i,
\]
\textit{where $\sum_i p_i=1_V$ and $p_ip_j=\delta_{ij}p_i$. Use this fact to prove the existence of the singular value decomposition for an arbitrary linear map between finite-dimensional complex vector spaces.}
\end{exercise}

A linear map $U\colon V\to V$ is \textbf{unitary} if $U^{-1}=U^*$, where the adjoint is determined by $\langle fv,w\rangle=\langle v,f^*w\rangle$.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $U\colon V\to V$ be unitary. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item $U$ preserves inner products, orthogonality, and rank-one projections.
\item Conjugation $a\mapsto U^{-1}aU$ preserves self-adjointness, sums, products, and spectra.
\item The transformations of states by $U$ and of observables by conjugation give equivalent descriptions of the same evolution.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

A scalar $\lambda\in k$ is an \textbf{eigenvalue} for a linear map $f\colon V\to V$ if there exists a nonzero vector $v\in V$ for which $fv=\lambda v$. Its \textbf{eigenspace} is
\[
\ker(f-\lambda 1_V),
\]
whose nonzero elements are eigenvectors; its dimension is the \textbf{geometric multiplicity} of $\lambda$.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $a$ and $b$ be self-adjoint endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional Hermitian vector space. Prove that if $ab=ba$, then every eigenspace of $a$ is preserved by $b$. Deduce that}
\[
V=\bigoplus_{\lambda,\mu}V_{\lambda,\mu}
\]
\textit{as an orthogonal direct sum of simultaneous eigenspaces.}
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let a group $G$ act linearly on $V$, and let $h\in\End(V)$ commute with this action. Prove that every eigenspace of $h$ is a representation of $G$. Explain why an irreducible representation of dimension greater than one contained in an eigenspace forces the corresponding eigenvalue to be degenerate.}
\end{exercise}

The \textbf{tensor algebra} and \textbf{exterior algebra} of $V$ are
\[
T(V)=\bigoplus_{k\geq0}V^{\otimes k},
\qquad
\Lambda V=T(V)/\langle v\otimes v:v\in V\rangle.
\]
Define $\Lambda^kV$ to be the degree-$k$ component of $\Lambda V$.

\begin{exercise}
\textit{If $V$ is finite dimensional, show that the highest nonzero graded component of $\Lambda V$ is one-dimensional.}
\end{exercise}

The \textbf{determinant} $\det(f)$ is the coefficient of the linear map on the highest-graded component of $\Lambda V$ given by
\[
v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{\dim(V)}\longmapsto
f(v_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge f(v_{\dim(V)}).
\]

\begin{exercise} \textit{Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\det(f\oplus g)=\det(f)\det(g)$.
\item If $V=\mathbb{R}^n$, then $\det(f)$ is the signed volume of $f(I^n)$ where $I=[0,1]$.
\item Cramer's rule.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

For $f\in\End(V)$, the \textbf{characteristic polynomial} is
\[
\lambda\longmapsto\det(f-\lambda 1_V).
\]

\begin{exercise}
\textit{Let $f$ be a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$. Assume that its characteristic polynomial splits over $k$. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Every eigenvalue of $f$ is a root of its characteristic polynomial.
\item The geometric multiplicity of each eigenvalue is at most its \textbf{algebraic multiplicity}, its multiplicity as a root of the characteristic polynomial.
\item $\trace(f)=\sum_i\lambda_i$ and $\det(f)=\prod_i\lambda_i$, where each eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ is repeated according to its algebraic multiplicity.
\item The operator $f$ satisfies its own characteristic polynomial.
\item When $k=\mathbb{C}$, the operator $f$ has a Jordan normal form.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

\section{Calculus}

A \textbf{smooth manifold} is a topological space that is locally diffeomorphic to Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$. A \textbf{smooth vector bundle} $p\colon E\to M$ consists of a smooth surjection $p$ between smooth manifolds $E$ and $M$ such that all fibers are isomorphic to a single vector space and smooth local trivializations exist. A prototypical example is the \textbf{tangent bundle} $TM$, which consists of all tangent spaces of points of a manifold. A \textbf{vector field} $X\in\Gamma(TM)$ is a smooth section of the tangent bundle.

\begin{exercise}
\textit{Write down precise intrinsic definitions for the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Smooth manifold, smooth map, vector bundle, and tangent bundle.
\item Bundle map, direct sum, tensor product, and dual bundle.
\item Derivative of a smooth function between manifolds.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

For a configuration manifold $M$, velocities are tangent vectors and linear measurements of velocities are cotangent vectors. An inner product may identify these spaces, but this identification is additional structure.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $p\in M$. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item A velocity at $p$ is naturally an element of $T_pM$, whereas momentum and force are naturally elements of $T_p^*M$.
\item The pairing $\alpha(v)$ for $\alpha\in T_p^*M$ and $v\in T_pM$ is invariant under changes of coordinates.
\item An inner product on $T_pM$ induces an isomorphism $T_pM\cong T_p^*M$, but no such isomorphism is canonical without additional structure.
\end{itemize}
Interpret the scalar $\alpha(v)$ as infinitesimal work when $\alpha$ is force and $v$ is displacement.
\end{exercise}

To define integration in a coordinate-free way, we integrate differential forms over chains. Write $\Omega^n(M)=\Gamma(\Lambda^nT^*M)$ for the space of \textbf{differential $n$-forms}. The \textbf{exterior derivative} is the natural graded derivation $d\colon\Omega^n(M)\to\Omega^{n+1}(M)$ extending the differential of smooth functions and satisfying $d^2=0$.

The de Rham cohomology of $M$ is
\[
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^n(M)=
\ker(d\colon\Omega^n(M)\to\Omega^{n+1}(M))/
\im(d\colon\Omega^{n-1}(M)\to\Omega^n(M)).
\]

The equation $dF=0$ is the coordinate-free homogeneous Maxwell equation.

\begin{exercise} \textit{Let $M$ be a four-dimensional spacetime and let $F\in\Omega^2(M)$. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item $F$ assigns a scalar flux to each oriented infinitesimal surface.
\item If $F=dA$ for some $A\in\Omega^1(M)$, then replacing $A$ by $A+d\lambda$ does not change $F$.
\item A global potential $A$ exists for a closed form $F$ iff $[F]=0$ in $H_{\mathrm{dR}}^2(M)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

A \textbf{singular $n$-simplex} is a smooth map $\sigma\colon\Delta^n\to M$. Singular $n$-simplices freely generate the abelian group $C_n(M)$ of \textbf{singular $n$-chains}. The \textbf{boundary map} $\partial\colon C_n(M)\to C_{n-1}(M)$ is defined by
\[
\partial\sigma=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j\sigma\circ f_j,
\]
where $f_j\colon\Delta^{n-1}\to\Delta^n$ includes the face opposite the $j$-th vertex.

A \textbf{chain complex} $(A_n,d_n)_n$ is a sequence of abelian groups and homomorphisms $d_n\colon A_n\to A_{n-1}$ such that $d_{n-1}\circ d_n=0$. Its $n$-th \textbf{homology group} is
\[
H_n=\ker(d_n)/\im(d_{n+1}).
\]
Singular homology is the homology of the singular chain complex. Cohomology and cochains are defined similarly, with coboundary maps increasing degree; singular cohomology is obtained by dualizing the singular chain complex.

\begin{exercise}
\textit{Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item Differential forms on $M$ form a cochain complex under the exterior derivative.
\item Define the integral of an $n$-form on an $n$-chain and prove $\int_c d\omega=\int_{\partial c}\omega$ for every $n$-chain $c$ and differential $(n-1)$-form $\omega$.
\item Singular cohomology with real coefficients and de Rham cohomology are naturally isomorphic.
\item Bounded chain complexes of finite-dimensional vector spaces form a symmetric monoidal category with duals; the trace of an endomorphism is its Lefschetz number and the dimension of a complex is its Euler characteristic.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise} \textit{Interpret}
\[
\int_{\partial c}\omega=\int_c d\omega
\]
\textit{as a relation between a quantity measured on a boundary and its source in the interior. Give conceptual proofs that the fundamental theorem of calculus, circulation--curl, flux--divergence, and Faraday induction are instances of this identity after the relevant geometric structures are chosen.}
\end{exercise}

An \textbf{affine connection} is a map $\nabla\colon\Gamma(TM)\times\Gamma(TM)\to\Gamma(TM)$ such that $\nabla_{fX}Y=f\nabla_XY$ and $\nabla_X(fY)=X(f)Y+f\nabla_XY$ for every smooth scalar field $f$ on $M$.

A symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold $M$ equipped with a closed nondegenerate two-form $\omega$. For $H\in C^\infty(M)$, the Hamiltonian vector field $X_H$ is determined by
\[
\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH.
\]

\begin{exercise} \textit{Suppose $\dim(M)=2n$. Prove the following:}
\begin{itemize}
\item $\omega^n/n!$ is a volume form.
\item Every diffeomorphism preserving $\omega$ preserves this volume form.
\item $H$ is constant along the flow of $X_H$.
\item An observable $f\in C^\infty(M)$ is conserved iff $df(X_H)=0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{exercise}
